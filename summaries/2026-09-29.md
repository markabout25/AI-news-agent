# AI Digest — September 29, 2026

## Key Themes
The AI world is intensely focused on the **agentic paradigm shift** and what it means for platform power — specifically, whether agents will become the new aggregation layer that displaces apps as the primary interface between users and services. Separately, the practical ML research community continues refining the foundations, with renewed attention on how modern architectures perform on bread-and-butter tasks like text classification.

## Top Stories

1. **[Apps, Agents, and Aggregation](https://stratechery.com/2026/apps-agents-and-aggregation/)** — *Stratechery, 2026-09-28*
Ben Thompson argues that AI agents represent the ultimate expression of Aggregation Theory: by acting as intermediaries that select and orchestrate apps on users' behalf, agents reduce apps to fungible back-end services. This reframes the entire tech landscape — whoever controls the agentic layer captures the most consequential distribution chokepoint since the smartphone home screen.

2. **[One More Note on Agents, Meta Connect, Meta Enterprise Platform](https://stratechery.com/2026/one-more-note-on-agents-meta-connect-meta-enterprise-platform/)** — *Stratechery, 2026-09-29*
Following Meta Connect, Thompson contends that Meta is uniquely positioned to own the *consumer* agentic layer (given its social graph and device ambitions) but is making a strategic error by chasing enterprise. The piece highlights how platform companies' instincts to go upmarket can blind them to the bigger prize sitting in their existing user base.

3. **[Language Models for Text Classification: From Bag-of-Words to Jev](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/classifier-history-and-jev)** — *Ahead of AI, 2026-09-29*
Sebastian Raschka provides a comprehensive visual walkthrough of the full arc of text classification — RNNs, CNNs, Transformers, and the newer Jev architecture — with hands-on benchmarks comparing accuracy, calibration, and efficiency. It's a valuable reference for practitioners deciding which model class actually fits their production constraints rather than defaulting to the largest available LLM.

## Takeaway
The most significant signal this cycle is Thompson's framing of **agents as the new aggregation layer** — it crystallizes an idea the industry has been circling for over a year and makes the strategic stakes concrete: the company that becomes the default agent intermediary won't just distribute software, it will *subsume* the app economy entirely.
